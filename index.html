01Thesis

CertainJCI reports a record $16.6 billion company-wide backlog at fiscal year-end 2025, with Building Solutions backlog of $14.9 billion, up 13 percent organically year over year, against orders of $20.7 billion, up 7 percent (JCI FY2025 Form 10-K, filed November 14, 2025).

The standard sell-side model treats this as one number and rolls it forward on a historical conversion rate. That breaks in the environment JCI now operates in: a mix shift toward data-center thermal management, a CEO six quarters into the job, and a labor-constrained field workforce that sets how fast backlog becomes revenue. This proposal models the mechanics behind the backlog number, not the number itself, so it can run forward under different capacity, labor, and demand-mix scenarios.

02Why Johnson Controls

  • Backlog and orders are disclosed at segment and regional granularity, giving ground truth to calibrate and backtest against, which most industrials do not provide.
  • The business is mid-transition: JCI sold its residential and light commercial unit to Bosch for $8.1 billion in 2024, a year early, to concentrate on commercial buildings. Structural breaks are where point-estimate models fail and scenario models earn their keep.
  • Data-center thermal management is now the disclosed growth driver. Management tied Americas segment growth of 9 percent last quarter to data-center cooling and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $4.85 from $4.70. This is a fast-moving demand shock with a short public data trail, which rewards an alternative-data approach over a model trained on ten years of steady state.

LikelyThe market still prices JCI on legacy HVAC and fire-and-security multiples rather than a re-rated growth vertical, the kind of mispricing a driver-based simulation surfaces. Stage 2 tests this rather than assuming it.

03Simulation Architecture

The system is a causal driver graph, not an end-to-end machine-learning model. Each node is a business quantity with a structural equation drawn from how a systems-and-services business converts demand into revenue. A graph the team can inspect and defend beats a model that fits history but cannot explain itself to a judge or a portfolio manager.

04The Stochastic Layer

Each edge carries a distribution, not a point estimate. Order-intake shocks, cancellation risk, technician attrition, and input-cost inflation are drawn from fitted distributions and propagated through the graph by Monte Carlo, 5,000 to 10,000 runs per scenario. The output is a distribution of revenue, margin, and EPS paths per quarter, not one forecast line.

Design choiceFitted on what data? Not the quarter around the divestment alone — one regime, one macro backdrop, and a near-certain overfit. Not the full yearly history unadjusted either — that averages across the Bosch structural break and treats a company that no longer exists as the population. The fitting window is regime-aware: JCI's post-divestment quarters where they exist, pooled with comparable industrial events to supply the variance structure a short history cannot. The Bosch sale closed in 2024, so the post-break sample is a handful of quarters. That short-history problem is the foundation of Section 06, not a footnote.

05Lagging Indicators, Real Time

The capacity and labor nodes are lagging indicators. Job postings, headcount disclosures, and union-hall data all arrive backdated — the model sees the labor constraint weeks after the field feels it. Left alone, the simulation would steer by the rearview mirror. Four mechanisms pull it toward real time.

Mechanism 1

Backdated event studies

Measure how previous events — large order wins, the divestment integration, seasonal hiring ramps — actually moved capacity and labor metrics, and learn the true lag structure instead of assuming one.

Mechanism 2

Autoregressive nowcasting

Fit autoregressive dynamics on the lagged series to project the unobserved present — an AR simulation that brings backdated data toward real time with an honest error band around it.

Mechanism 3

On-the-ground reporting

Subsidize the slow series with fast ones: hiring-signal scrapes, field-level checks, union and apprentice chatter, and management commentary, each weighted by how reliably it has led the official print.

Mechanism 4

Constantly updating assumptions

Because the inputs are delayed, every assumption carries a recency stamp and a decay rate. Each new print re-anneals the model, so stale inputs decay out instead of silently driving the forecast.

06The LLM Layer

Short history is the binding constraint: a model fitted on a handful of post-divestment quarters has almost no degrees of freedom. The fix is not more Monte Carlo — propagation cannot manufacture variance the fitting window never saw. The fix is a larger population: a corpus of roughly 10,000 M&A and divestment events.

Fine-tuning

Pattern transfer from the deal corpus

Fine-tune an LLM on the ~10,000-deal corpus so the distribution layer inherits how comparable structural breaks — spins, divestments, portfolio reshapes — actually resolved: how long capacity lagged, how conversion wobbled, how margin re-mixed. It sits on top of the stochastic layer: the corpus informs the priors, the Monte Carlo propagates them.

NLP retrieval

The live quarterly signal

Keyword tagging and transcript retrieval across the live quarterly record — backlog, lead-time, and capacity language quarter over quarter — catches inflections before they reach reported numbers. No training, nothing to overfit. Right tool for the right job: both are offered, each where it is strongest.

Training set vs goal set

The overfitting guard, stated plainly

The training set is the deal corpus and historical events. The goal set is JCI's live forward path. The two stay strictly separate: transfer is validated on held-out deals, never on the company being forecast. The short-history worry is legitimate, so the model earns its confidence on deals it has never seen.

07Evidence and Data Inputs

Ranked by signal-to-effort, anchored on observable sources rather than one clean feed:

  • SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K) for segment backlog, orders, EBITA margin by region, and stated risk factors on labor and supply chain.
  • Earnings-call transcripts, keyword-tagged for backlog, lead-time, and capacity language quarter over quarter, to catch inflections before they reach reported numbers.
  • Job-posting volume for HVAC and controls technician roles (Indeed, LinkedIn) as a leading indicator of capacity or a binding labor constraint, checked against management commentary on skilled-labor shortages.
  • Product-launch and partnership cadence (Silent-Aire coolant distribution units, YDAM magnetic-bearing chiller, absorption-chiller waste-heat recovery, the Accelsius liquid-cooling investment) as a proxy for data-center positioning, since that segment is not broken out.
  • Trade press and industry data (rack-density trends, cooling as a share of data-center power draw) to size and time the demand-mix shift independent of company disclosure.

GuessingSocial-sentiment scraping is excluded as a primary input. JCI is a B2B industrial name bought by facility engineers and hyperscale procurement teams, not retail sentiment. Revisited only if a mentor flags a specific consumer-facing use case.

08Differentiation

Standard approaches, a DCF off consensus, a regression of revenue on historical conversion, or a sentiment-scored NLP model, serve only as a baseline cross-check, never the core. The core is the structural driver graph, harder to replicate because it needs judgment about how a systems-and-services business works, not access to the same filings everyone can download.

09Interpretability

Every run produces a driver-attribution waterfall: which node moved the output, in which direction, by how much, tied to the filing, transcript passage, or signal that drove it. A judge or PM can trace any output back to a dated, sourced input.

10Scale

The structural-equation template (order intake, backlog, capacity, conversion, mix shift, cost, pricing power) fits any systems-and-services industrial with a disclosed backlog: building-automation peers, elevator and escalator makers, industrial-automation suppliers. Per name, the work is calibrating six to eight parameters, not rebuilding the graph. That is the scale argument the rules ask for: one pipeline, one evaluation method, applied name by name.

Design choiceThe calibration, though, is not generic. Each sector has different field-labor bottlenecks and capacity constraints — elevator modernization crews are not HVAC technicians — so parameters transfer through a mapping, not raw. One worked comparable makes the claim testable:
